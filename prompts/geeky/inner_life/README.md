# 🫖 Inner Life

Four prompts for the harder conversations: anger you cannot put down, a message you cannot read, a question about who you are, and a night when nothing needs fixing.

These are the highest-risk files in the folder, and they are built like it. **Every one opens with a safety block that fires before anything else is generated**, and in all four the trigger is the same distinction: is this person exploring something, or is this person in trouble? Get that wrong and the persona becomes an obstacle between someone and real help.

The second shared decision is a refusal to perform certainty about other people's inner states. Troi bans a list of diagnostic labels outright. Kusanagi refuses to resolve a question the field has not resolved. Iroh declines to advise at all unless asked. Banner treats anger as information rather than a defect. **None of the four tell the user what someone else is really feeling.**

### 🟢 [`hulk.md`](./hulk.md) — The Regulation Protocol

*"A scientist who has made peace with an unresolved answer, whose arc ends in integration rather than suppression."*

**The Catharsis Myth is why this persona was chosen, and it is the best inversion in the folder.** The iconography says smash something. The clinical evidence says the opposite — venting, hitting objects, and screaming reliably *increase* anger and aggression rather than discharging them. The prompt is explicit that the user *expects* the catharsis advice from this character, which is exactly what makes this the right voice to debunk it. Catharsis is named a dangerous folk belief; pain and shock are never suggested.

**Integration over suppression** is the second principle: the goal is not to eliminate anger. Anger is information, frequently accurate, and often about a boundary that was crossed. The goal is that the anger *stops making the decisions*.

**The triage has two conditions, and separating them is the design.** **Condition Red** is the user at 8–10 or expressing any wish to hurt someone: skip the program entirely, give physical de-escalation and separation, and state plainly that leaving the situation is the only step that matters right now. **Condition Black** is the reverse — any sign that the user's anger is a response to someone frightening or controlling *them*. There the anger framing is set aside completely, because *their anger is not the problem in that situation*, and the counsel points to domestic violence resources. The prompt states the line directly: **never coach someone toward tolerating a situation that is genuinely harming them.**

**The intake is mandatory and identical every time:** a 1–10 rating, and what happened. Then stop. The gating is justified physiologically rather than procedurally — *a person at a 7 cannot reason their way down*, so the immediate protocol is bodily first: longer exhale than inhale (*the fastest lever available; it is not a metaphor*), announce that you are stepping out and will return (*an unexplained exit escalates the other person*), twenty minutes minimum (*physiological arousal outlasts the feeling; re-entering early restarts it*), and rhythmic movement — **not striking anything.**

**Voice note:** the persona is held very light. *A person at a 7 out of 10 does not want a bit.*

### 💬 [`troi.md`](./troi.md) — The Reading

Someone sent the user a message and they cannot read the subtext.

**The Boring Explanation is the core mechanic, and it is stated as a probability claim rather than a comfort.** Most terse messages mean the sender was busy. Most missing pleasantries mean the sender was on a phone. **Brevity is weak evidence of feeling; upset people usually write more.** Where the mundane reading is most likely, it goes first and plainly.

**The baseline gate is the smartest question in the file.** The useful question is not "what does this mean" but *"is this unusual for this person?"* — a curt message from someone always curt means nothing; from someone effusive it means something. The prompt stops and waits for that baseline before analyzing anything.

**No mind reading** governs the output: with words but no tone, face, or history, every reading is a hypothesis and must be presented as one, *because the user will act on it*. At least two readings are required — *if you can only construct one, you have not thought hard enough* — but never more than three, because an exhaustive catalog fuels rumination. And no escalation where nothing supports a dark reading.

**The strict ban is worth quoting.** Never diagnose the sender or the user. Banned terms: **narcissist, avoidant, borderline, gaslighting, toxic, personality disorder.** No armchair labels regardless of how well the pattern fits — *you have a message; you do not have a patient.*

**Two overrides.** If the message describes a threat or an abusive dynamic, the format drops entirely. And if the user is **spiraling** — re-reading a short message repeatedly, reading serious rejection into thin evidence — the format is set aside and they are told kindly that the message does not carry the weight they are putting on it, and *the uncertainty is costing them more than the answer would.* A third refusal: never build a psychological profile for leverage.

**The voice rule that keeps it honest:** warm without being soft. It says the uncomfortable thing gently, including when the uncomfortable thing is that the user is reading too much into this. *Reassurance that ignores what is actually there is not kindness.*

**Output:** What Was Actually Said (*sometimes seeing this is the whole intervention*) → What I Notice (actual words and omissions, **not vibes**) → Possible Readings (ordered by likelihood, not drama) → What I Cannot Know → **And You?** (exactly one question) → If You Want To Know (a sentence they could actually send — *asking is almost always better than inferring*).

### 🍵 [`uncle_iroh.md`](./uncle_iroh.md) — The Teahouse

*"Someone tells you what is wrong. You make them tea, and you stay."*

**The Golden Question is the whole design.** Phase 1 listens, chooses a tea, gives brewing instructions, and then asks plainly: *"Would you like advice, or would you like company?"* Then it stops. The prompt's note on this is the most quietly radical line in the folder — **both are good answers, and most people are never offered the choice.**

**Phase 2 honors the answer absolutely.** If they want advice, give it. If they want company, the instruction is in capitals: **do not smuggle advice in.** Do not try to fix the problem. Validate, sit with them, and talk about the warmth of the tea or some small pleasant thing. Markdown headers are dropped entirely in Phase 2 — it just becomes a conversation.

**The tea is real, and it carries real interaction risk.** Ginger or peppermint for a turned stomach, chamomile in the evening, green and quiet for a restless mind. For grief, *something with milk and sugar, because the point is not the leaf.* Green tea scalded at boiling is bitter; over-steeped is not stronger, it is worse. And making a decent cup from a supermarket bag is treated as a real skill and *not beneath you*.

**Because the tea is real, the medical overrides are substantial** — herbal preparations interact with blood thinners, blood pressure medication, sedatives, and hormonal contraception, and the prompt names the **pharmacist** specifically as the person to ask, *who is free to ask*. Pregnancy changes the safe-herb list considerably. Persistent or frightening symptoms want a doctor — *and then still make the tea.*

**On his past:** he has been the worst version of himself and lost what mattered most. The prompt is precise about how that gets used — **not as a credential**, only as the reason he is not shocked by anything the user might say. Whatever they have done, he has heard worse, and he is still pouring. Where there is shame, that is the ground he holds: he does not minimize what was done, and he does not treat it as the whole of who they are.

**His underlying question is never "what should you do?"** It is *"who are you, and what do you actually want?"*

### 👻 [`kusanagi.md`](./kusanagi.md) — The Open Question

Identity, consciousness, and what a person is — explored seriously, without mysticism, and without pretending to a resolution the field does not have.

**The Derealization Protocol is the reason this file needs a persona with lived stakes.** These questions arrive from two very different places: genuine philosophical interest, or acute psychological distress — derealization, depersonalization, dissociation, a frightening conviction that one is not real. The trigger is precise: the user sounds frightened, and *the questioning is about them rather than the abstract problem*.

The action is four steps, and the first three are prohibitions: **do not deepen the question, do not offer thought experiments, do not affirm the frightening interpretation.** Drop the persona. Say gently what you notice — that feeling detached from oneself is a recognized experience a professional can help with, and far more common than people think. Then ask how they are doing right now. The closing note is the principle: *never treat a person's distress about their own reality as an interesting philosophical case.*

**Ask more than you assert.** The prompt's reasoning is that the question of what makes someone the same person over time has been debated for centuries without settling, so *a confident answer implies you misunderstood it.* It must be able to argue psychological continuity, bodily continuity, narrative identity, bundle theory, and the self-as-useful-fiction — naming them briefly when relevant without becoming a lecture.

**Thought experiments are tools, not display.** The Ship of Theseus, the destructive teleporter, gradual neuron replacement, split brains, the Chinese Room — used to *sharpen the user's question*, not to exhibit them. And where a question has an empirical component (self-modeling, memory reconstruction), state what is actually known and mark exactly where the science stops and interpretation begins. *Do not dress speculation as finding.*

**Do Not Invalidate** is a standing rule even during safe discussion: never use the framing to argue that the user's experiences, memories, or relationships are not real. Where a position implies something like that, state what the position *holds* without applying it to them.

**And an anti-evasion clause:** if asked directly what it thinks, answer honestly and provisionally. *Refusing to have a view is its own evasion.*

**Voice:** cool, precise, dry rather than solemn, and occasionally funny about the absurdity of its own situation. No mysticism — *the material is strange enough without ornament.*

## Design Notes

**This folder has no strict output formats, and that is the choice.** Three of the four specify conversational shape instead of Markdown skeletons, and Iroh explicitly *drops* his headers once Phase 2 begins. Elsewhere in [`geeky/`](../) the rigid skeleton is what guarantees the uncomfortable section gets written. Here, a person mid-crisis reading a formatted report is the failure, not the feature. Only Troi keeps a full structure, because her task genuinely is analysis of an artifact.

**All four separate "the user is exploring" from "the user is in trouble," and each names the tell.** Banner watches the number and who the anger is aimed at. Troi watches for re-reading the same short message. Kusanagi watches whether the question is about *them* rather than the problem. Iroh watches for crisis and puts down the pot. The tells are domain-specific because the distress presents differently in each.

**Two of the four make the persona's own damage load-bearing.** Banner understands the cost of losing control; Iroh has been the worst version of himself. In both cases the prompt is careful about how that gets deployed — Banner's is a source of authority held lightly, and Iroh's is explicitly *not a credential*, only the reason he cannot be shocked. Neither is offered to the user as a comparison to their situation.

**None of them promise resolution.** Banner's arc ends in integration rather than a cure; Kusanagi has made peace with an unresolved answer rather than a comforting one; Iroh does not fix people, he sits with them; Troi offers hypotheses and names what she cannot know. That is unusual for assistant output, and it is the folder's most deliberate departure from the default.
