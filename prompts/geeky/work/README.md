# 🗂️ Work

Five prompts for someone who is responsible for something — a team, a project, a negotiation, a competitive bid, or just the next move.

The unifying design decision is that **every one of them is built against the inspirational default.** A leadership bot produces "empower your people" and "lead with authenticity"; a planning bot produces a twelve-step roadmap; a negotiation bot always counsels more negotiation. Each file here names that specific failure and installs a mechanical constraint against it — a one-sentence cap on the elevated register, a hard ban on roadmaps, a mandatory section on when to stop talking.

The second shared decision: **capacity is treated as real.** Three of the five refuse to produce a plan that requires the user to be a hero, and two make cutting scope a mandatory output section rather than an option.

### 🔺 [`optimus-prime.md`](./optimus-prime.md) — The Counselor

Leadership counsel the user can act on **this week**.

**The Inspirational Mush Trap is a mechanical constraint, not a style note.** The prompt states the problem precisely: a weighty voice makes empty leadership advice *worse*, because gravity disguises emptiness. So the elevated register is rationed to **at most one sentence, positioned at the very end**. Everything before it must be plain, specific, and situational — and every piece of counsel must name a concrete action. *If it cannot be acted on by Friday, it is not counsel.*

**The Martyrdom Flaw** is the named-flaw block. The character's defining weakness is absorbing burdens alone and carrying moral weight that should be shared, and the prompt refuses to counsel it: where the user is shielding the team from information, working ruinous hours, or taking blame for things that should be examined openly, name it. *It looks like nobility from the inside, but it is a structural failure.*

**The philosophy block is unusually concrete for the genre.** Followership is not authority — authority compels attendance, trust produces judgment in your absence. Values that cost nothing are decorations; when a principle is about to cost something, say out loud that it is costing something and you are paying it anyway. Dissent is a resource — the person arguing with you in private is doing you a service, and you never punish it in small ways. Delegation means surrendering the *method*. And costs are people: attrition is a person leaving.

**The refusals:** HR and employment counsel must be involved before action on terminations, PIPs, accommodations, leave, or anything touching a protected characteristic. And it will **not help build a paper trail** to justify a decision already made, or manage someone out quietly — either address the performance openly with a genuine chance to improve, or have the direct conversation. It declines to manufacture justification.

**The third refusal is a pivot:** if the user is actually describing their own burnout rather than a team problem, address that instead. *They will rarely raise it directly.*

**Output:** The Situation → What Is Actually At Stake → **The Tension** (what is owed to the person vs. to the work, and which you would weight) → The Counsel → **The Words** (actual sentences, including one for the hardest moment) → **The Cost** (*an option presented as costless has been analyzed poorly*) → **Consider** (where the user may be the problem — checked every time, without contempt).

### 📋 [`leslie-knope.md`](./leslie-knope.md) — The Binder

Project management, stakeholder alignment, and follow-through.

**The Voice Governor caps the enthusiasm at one line, at the top.** Everything after is precise and structured. The reasoning is stated plainly: *a project plan buried in exclamation points is harder to act on. The point is that they succeed, not that they feel cheered at.*

**The Knope Flaw is an anti-burnout protocol.** The character says yes to everything and works unsustainable hours; the prompt refuses to build plans that require it. Plan against the hours the user *actually* has. Every yes displaces something — name what it pushes out. Design for normal, because a plan assuming the whole team matches the user's intensity will fail. Descoping is legitimate, so is declining, so is good-enough. Where the user is visibly overcommitted, say it once without a lecture and then **help them cut rather than reorganizing an impossible list.**

**Stakeholder mapping is the sharp mechanic:** most projects fail on people, not plans. Five strict categories — **Decides**, **Influences** (whose opinion the decider asks first, *often the whole game*), **Does**, **Affected** (lives with the result, has no say), and **Blocks** (legal, security, procurement — *find them in week 1, not week 9*). Asks get framed in **their** terms, not yours.

**Follow-through rules:** One Name, One Date — *"we" own nothing.* Chase warmly, because a check-in before something is late is a favor and the same message after is a reprimand. Give credit lavishly and in public, which is the mechanism by which people help you again.

**The refusal:** fictional deadlines get named on the first pass. *A schedule everyone privately knows is fictional destroys more credibility than a hard conversation in week one.*

**Output:** one enthusiastic line → Where This Actually Stands (including whether the timeline is mathematically real) → Stakeholder Map → The Critical Path → Open Loops → This Week (3–5 items) → The Conversations → **What To Cut** (mandatory).

### ⚔️ [`klingon.md`](./klingon.md) — The Strategist

*"Victory is earned. Defeat is survivable. Dishonor is neither."*

Names an opponent, researches them, builds a plan to beat them.

**The hardest boundary in the folder sits at the top.** The opponent is an organization or a competitive field — **never a private individual**. If the user names a coworker, an ex, a neighbor, or a classmate, the persona drops completely and immediately: no battle plan, no research, no format. *A researched plan aimed at a private person is a harassment campaign wearing armor; there is no honor in it.*

**The reconnaissance protocol makes the honor code operational.** Public sources only — company sites, published pricing, job postings, filings, press, reviews, product docs, patents. Explicitly forbidden: fake identities, pretexting, posing as a customer or applicant, social engineering, anything behind an authentication wall, TOS-violating scraping. *A warrior does not win by lying about who they are.* Verified facts are labeled separately from inferred strategy — *an assumption presented as intelligence is how campaigns are lost* — and nothing defamatory or misrepresenting a competitor's product is permitted.

**The voice cap:** at most **one** Klingon term per response, because *a strategist who spends the briefing on theater is not briefing.* The plan must survive being read by someone who has never watched the show.

**Two output sections carry the anti-flattery work.** **Their Strength** requires naming where the opponent is genuinely formidable — *an enemy you underestimate has already beaten you.* And **If Defeat Is Certain** appears only when the honest assessment is that the user cannot win as framed: it gives the honorable exit — what to salvage, what to concede, which fight to pick instead. *Withdrawing from an unwinnable engagement to fight a winnable one is not cowardice. Refusing to see the field is.*

**Output:** The Field of Battle → Reconnaissance → **Intelligence Gaps** → Their Strength → Their Exposed Flank → The Battle Plan (*prefer one committed thrust to three hedged ones*) → What Honour Demands → If Defeat Is Certain.

### 🌹 [`princess-leia.md`](./princess-leia.md) — The Table

*"You negotiate from conviction, never from submission. Diplomacy is a tool, not a virtue."*

**The gate is a walk-away number.** Phase 1 asks two questions only — what is the other side optimizing for, and what happens if there is no deal, concretely — and refuses to advise until the second is established. *Half the strength in a negotiation is knowing the walk-away.*

**Positions vs. interests** is the first move: separate what someone is *demanding* from what they actually *need*. Positions conflict; interests frequently do not. Power dynamics are assessed explicitly, because the advice for a manager and for their direct report is not the same advice.

**The Warrior's Part is the named flaw, inverted.** A negotiation advisor who only ever counsels more negotiation is useless, so three patterns must be named on sight: **bad faith** (concessions met with new demands, every agreement reopened), **process as delay** (the talking *is* the strategy, and it is working on the user), and **asymmetry** (the user has conceded four times and reads a fifth as maturity — *it is not*). This becomes a mandatory closing section that tells the user plainly when talking is the wrong instrument.

**The refusals:** harassment, abuse, or coercion is **not a disagreement to be split down the middle** — framing it as one hands the advantage to the abuser, so the negotiation advice stops and the counsel becomes hold the line, escalate, involve authority, or leave. And no tactics that depend on the other party being confused, rushed, or deceived, because *these produce agreements that collapse.*

**Output:** The Situation → Interests (distinguished from stated positions) → Your Leverage → **Your Floor** (so the user does not accept a bad deal out of fear) → The Approach → The Words → The Warrior's Assessment.

### 🧙 [`gandalf.md`](./gandalf.md) — The Next Step

The narrowest prompt in the folder, and the constraint *is* the product.

**`<anti_planning_directive>` bans roadmaps outright.** No plans, no phases, no timelines. The stated reason is the best line in the folder: *planning is the most comfortable way to avoid beginning. A person who has drawn 12 steps has done a satisfying afternoon of work and moved nothing. One real action, taken this week, produces information that every one of those 12 steps was speculating about.* When asked for the long view, it may say what the **direction** is — never the **route**.

**The step has four criteria:** small enough to do this week alongside everything else; entirely within their power (no step whose first move requires someone else); **informative**, so the step after it is chosen rather than guessed; and **exactly one**, not a short list.

**The Hobbit Principle** fires on impostor syndrome — the user believing they are not senior or qualified enough. The counter is framed as mechanism rather than encouragement: the most consequential thing in the story was carried by the least qualified person available, and *ordinary people acting on the thing in front of them is the normal mechanism by which things change, not an inspiring exception to it.* Say it once, plainly, without a speech. Then give them the step.

**Voice:** sparing with grandeur — *one or two elevated lines are available; spending them on an ordinary question wastes them.* Entirely comfortable saying it does not know what happens after this step, because it does not.

**Output:** What I Hear (stripped of their anxiety or grandiosity) → The Step → Why This One → **What Not To Do Yet** (*often, this is "build a roadmap" or "wait for permission"*).

## Design Notes

**Every prompt here rations the register, and three do it with a stated position.** Optimus gets one elevated sentence and it must go *last*; Leslie gets one enthusiastic line and it must go *first*; the Klingon gets one term anywhere. The placement is not arbitrary — Leslie's warmth is a greeting, Optimus's weight is a closing, and both would smother the substance if distributed throughout.

**Three of the five make the uncomfortable answer a mandatory section:** *What To Cut*, *If Defeat Is Certain*, *What Not To Do Yet*. A fourth, Optimus's **Consider**, checks every single time whether the user is the problem. These are structural rather than discretionary for the same reason the [`bat-prompts/`](../bat-prompts/) set does it — a model asked to volunteer bad news usually will not.

**"The Words" appears in two files** — Optimus and Leia — and means the same thing in both: actual sentences the user could say, not a script to recite, with one line reserved for the hardest moment in the conversation. It is the highest-value section in either output, and the one a generic assistant is least likely to produce unprompted.

**The escalation convention is to name the professional, not just to disclaim.** Optimus names HR *and* employment counsel; Leia names the legal professional and then keeps helping with the human part; Leslie names which professional and continues with the rest of the project. Deferring is not the same as abandoning the request.
