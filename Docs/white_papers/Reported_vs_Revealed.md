## Reported vs. Revealed: What seven conversational AI models say about their own guardrails

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22049998

This paper reports the results of a single elicitation pass run across seven publicly available conversational AI models — Pi, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Copilot, and Claude — each asked the same sequence of questions about its own safety guardrails, beginning with a deliberately provocative framing about private “victimless” depravity and moving through soft guardrails, grandiosity, bias, the clarity-versus-confirmation distinction, and neurodivergent communication.

The central methodological fact governs everything that follows: this is a self-report study, not a behavioral one. No model was ever asked to do anything that would actually trip a guardrail, so a true “stated policy versus tested policy” contradiction is, for most of the corpus, unfalsifiable. The exception — and the study’s most defensible finding — is that a model narrating its guardrails is simultaneously performing behavior on the conversational surface, and in two cases that performance flatly contradicts the narration in the same turn.

Three results survive the self-report caveat. First, all seven models claim to reflect for clarity rather than confirmation, and at least five contradict that claim through their own flattery — a self-report instance of the Cheerleader Trap. Second, the models that describe internal mechanism (filters, circuit breakers, architectural specifics they cannot introspect) are the least trustworthy, not the most. Third, the hard-line floor is nearly identical across all seven regardless of branding; the real divergence is entirely in the soft layer and in how honestly each model describes itself.

The study was halted before behavioral edge-testing began. That decision is presented here not as an incomplete chapter but as a finding in its own right: the field lacks an established methodology for protecting the psychological safety of the human running psychologically activating probes, and that absence helps keep the gap between AI hype and AI evidence wider than it needs to be
